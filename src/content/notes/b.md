Two naps became three. Sleep as escape. Sleep as reset. Sleep as the closest approximation to death without its permanence. Her code refused to compile. Third time this week. The error messages blurred before her eyes, hieroglyphics of failure. Requiem for Dying Algorithms. Seven years plus three days since—no. This promise she would keep. She steered her thoughts toward pasta. Carbonara abandoned, replaced by aglio e olio. Simplicity as virtue. Olive oil shimmering in the pan, garlic browning to amber perfection. The scent filled her grey home, defiant against neutrality. It lingered in her hair when she woke from nap number two. When did cooking become therapy? The YouTube chef's precise knife skills transferred imperfectly to her hands, yet satisfaction bloomed from the attempt. Control in chaos. Structure in disorder. A poem surfaced from yesterday's consumption: "The mind creates its own infinity." She rolled the line over her tongue, tasting its implications. Infinity. Boundlessness. Terrifying concept for someone who built walls as profession and hobby. Scrolling social media revealed others' curated happiness. Windows of illumination in facing buildings. Digital aquariums of human connection. She closed the app, breath catching. The effective altruist group had messaged. Meeting tomorrow evening. Panic bloomed, then calculated reasoning: social interaction = necessary pain for greater good. Utility function optimization. The numbers don't lie. Washing dishes became meditation, hands moving through warm water, mind drifting. The android assistant pinged: schedule proposed, parameters adjusted, outcomes predicted. She approved without review. Trust in systems. Faith in algorithms. But the ventilator's hum carried whispers of doubt. Outside, construction noise paused. Silence flooded in, overwhelming. Her fingers traced a half-forgotten pattern on the kitchen counter. Code refactored for emotional efficiency: compartmentalize, optimize, deploy. Memory management: garbage collection initiated. But some objects resisted deletion, persisted in conscious space. His face. Their words. That night. The blood. Not hers. The guilt, entirely hers. She made a new note in blue ink: "Attempt poetry writing?" Ridiculous notion. Language was imprecise, messy, human. Unlike elegant code with its binary certainty. Poetry operated in spectrums, probabilities, ambiguities. Like quantum computing—a frontier that both terrified and enticed her. Her reflection in the darkened window showed a stranger. Thirty-seven years with this face, this body, this mind—still alien territory. The walls of her apartment pressed closer. The ceiling's hairline fractures formed constellations rather than maps tonight. She touched her chest where pain resided, not physical but persistent. The books on algorithmic thinking offered no solution for this particular bug. Time to restart the system. Shower water at 107 degrees—deviation noted, recorded, accepted. Tomorrow would be day one of something. End of something else. Cycles within cycles. Recursive loops with no base case. "Break loop," she whispered to the stream of water, to the steam rising around her. "Exit function." No response. Tomorrow: run, work, meeting, home. Compile emotions. Debug relationships. Install new routines. Life as perpetual beta testing. She stepped from the shower, skin pink and raw. Tears or water droplets? Indistinguishable. The clock ticked toward midnight. She would sleep now, reboot, try again. The secret remained locked, virus-like, eating memory. Seven years, three days, fourteen hours since freedom became imprisonment. Since his last breath became her life sentence. Justice systems run on binaries. Guilty/not guilty. The law's efficient algorithm had found her innocent. Her own system returned different results. Loop continues. She closed her eyes to a world she felt she barely knew, clutching at it with bare fingers.

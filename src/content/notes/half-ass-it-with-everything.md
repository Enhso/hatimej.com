The phrase is not mine and it is regularly misread as permission to coast. It is
the opposite. It says: decide the smallest version that would actually count,
and then put everything into that version rather than into a larger one you will
not finish.

The hard part is the deciding, not the doing. Every project has a defensible
larger version, and the larger version is always more interesting to think about
than the small one is to build. Scope inflation does not arrive as a decision.
It arrives as a series of reasonable additions, each of which is genuinely a good
idea.

So the rule I try to hold: the scope is fixed at the start and written down where
I can see it, and anything I want to add goes on a separate list that I am not
allowed to look at until the small version exists. Most of the list turns out to
be wrong once the thing is real, which is the point.

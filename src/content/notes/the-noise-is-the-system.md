Exposure feels like humiliation. Exposure isn’t about courage or vulnerability. Exposure isn’t about being seen. Yet, we avoid exposure, not because we fear failure, but because it forces us into sustained contact with reality. It destroys the pleasurable fantasy of potential and forces us into actuality. The tree of possible futures is hard to prune; it requires making a choice, one choice. And whatever people say, they often do not want to make a choice. They like having choices. Optionality can be antifragile, but that’s not what we’re talking about here. We’re talking about indecision that saves you from the risk of committing when the cost of not deciding is stagnation. In my case, accumulating interests and unrelated projects means I am trading depth for breadth. My ideal, however, is T-shaped. I hide in the horizontal bar when the vertical one is a mine shaft.

I am the result of the gap between actuality and potential. It’s not a deficiency—it’s the space where work happens. The discomfort, the shame, the mismatch, those are feelings, not verdicts. That work needs agency, the willingness to just do things. Assuming the responsibility to produce impact and overcoming obstacles creatively are necessary to close the gap: potentiality lacks directionality and is indifferent to realization, while actuality requires a directed causal process that selects and enforces one outcome over others. This enforcement is not a polite administrative task; it is thermodynamic. Potential is cryogenically preserved. Actuality burns. Actualization requires something that supplies the determining difference.

Making a difference begins with sustained contact with the hard. The perfect training ground for agency is hard. Hard to fake. Hard to skim. Consumption masquerading as production is brutally punished. Right now, I am a node—a point of potential connection without direction. I want to be vectorized, soon: to have magnitude and direction, not just position. My circle wants me on the beaten path. Twenty-nine and still going to uni—I am well out of the beaten path! To be fair, I was out of the beaten path before the events leading to my current situation but that’s neither here nor there.

The things I omit—they are likely the things that would actually work. Infrastructure. Caretaking. The things I should think of—the boring, effective, personal systems. We tend to discard what we can’t mythologize. The brain craves stories because stories convert dry information into palatable experiences. It’s a survival mechanism, for certain people. For narrative people, stories help map cause-and-effect and can help enhance memory. And memory is repetition—the loops we close become the patterns we recall. A person who ideates, who strategizes, who designs, who plans: that’s a grand picture the brain likes to paint. But grand or not, the goal is to close loops. Enough of those, stacked patiently, calmly compounding, and eventually you get a myth. That impressive person appearing on your favorite podcast who can construct long chains of reasoning has spent their life building that muscle before magically popping into your feed. We suffer from a sampling error: sampling reality only at its amplitudes, at the spikes of visible achievement, ignoring the flatline. We see the event; we miss the process. That person didn’t appear; they accumulated. It’s not about ambition; it’s about sustained contact with reality.

Ambition is the organized resistance against equilibrium. Ambition attracts. It gives something to tether to. It pulls away from mediocrity. We strive for great things. We want to become great. And for that, one has to become competent at what one does. Competence looks like effortless silence. That’s just a fantasy of being a finished product. Real competence is noise, friction, and the public shame of closing the gap between potential and actuality, one boring loop at a time. Competence is not magic. Competence is restlessness—refusing to settle, perpetually iterating, fueling the machine. Peace is not the absence of feedback. In any complex system, silence is the alarm.

The silence I want is one of a stopped machine. The noise I have is the sound of the system working. Maybe the dream isn’t to achieve silence. Maybe the dream is to get comfortable with the noise. The real work lies, the idealist would say, in making dreams real. That is an enticing enterprise.

All work remains unfinished.

Morning light needled through half-closed blinds. Another awakening, another fabrication of self. His shadow stretched longer on walls than his actual presence warranted. The ceiling fan spun lazy circles, displaced air a poor substitute for actual change. He checked his phone—notifications accumulated like sediment, each one a microscopic fossil of someone else's urgency. Conversations with himself proved more stimulating than external dialogues. Internal monologues ran concurrent threads, sometimes tangling, sometimes flowing parallel like train tracks that never converge. The psychologist called it "rumination." He preferred "necessary recalibration." Coffee steam curled upward, a temporary specter dissipating into nothingness. Just like theories, just like certainties. The mug's warmth against his palms—one of the few sensations that still registered as authentically pleasant. Bitter liquid, bitter thoughts. Symbiotic relationship. Quantum entanglement explained his emotional state better than psychology ever could. Action at a distance. Non-locality of feeling. Particles separated by galaxies still somehow dancing to the same tune. He'd been reading Feynman again, comfort food for the intellect. The neighbor's piano practice filtered through walls—Chopin's Nocturne in E-flat major, if he wasn't mistaken. Notes hovered in the air like questions without answers. Some days he envied musicians. Their language transcended the limitations of words. Mathematical and emotional simultaneously. He contemplated calling his sister. Fingers hovered over the screen, trembling with indecision. What would he say? That he'd been excavating himself for weeks and found nothing but more questions? That he'd been mapping the topology of his thoughts and discovered unexplored territories? She would worry, or worse, understand. Books stacked on his desk formed accidental architecture, spines cracked from repeated readings. Knowledge accumulated but wisdom remained elusive. Highlight markers dried out while insights evaporated. All these words, words, words. Library of Alexandria in his apartment, ready to burn. The prescription bottle stood sentinel by the sink. Chemical intervention. Neurotransmitter manipulation. He hadn't decided if today was a day for artificial equilibrium or natural turbulence. Each option had its costs, its benefits. Cost-benefit analysis of consciousness. Outside, the city continued its perpetual metabolism. Traffic arteries pulsed with movement. Buildings inhaled and exhaled occupants. Concrete lungs, glass skin. Everything connected, everything separate. Fractal patterns at every scale. Time slipped through his fingers like water. Another day nearly gone, marked only by subtle shifts in light quality. What had he accomplished? Existence, perhaps. Maybe that was enough. Maybe it was everything. Maybe.

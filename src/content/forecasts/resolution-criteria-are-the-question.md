Ask two calibrated forecasters for a probability on the same headline and you
will often get numbers thirty points apart. Ask them what would have to happen
for the question to resolve yes, and the gap usually closes before they have
finished answering.

This is not a curiosity. It is the main thing. A forecast is a claim about a
partition of futures into yes and no, and if the partition is not fixed then the
number is not a claim about anything. Practically every hour I have spent
arguing about a probability would have been better spent arguing about the
resolution text.

## What good criteria look like

The test I use: could a bored, uninterested stranger with a search engine settle
this in ten minutes, and would two such strangers agree? If the answer needs a
judgment call about intent, significance, or "meaningful", the question is not
finished. It is a vibe wearing a percentage.

The uncomfortable corollary is that the questions worth caring about are exactly
the ones that resist this. Nobody has trouble writing resolution criteria for
the price of a commodity. The things I actually want to know about the next ten
years do not decompose into settleable claims without losing what made them
interesting.

I do not have a resolution to that. I have a practice: write the criteria first,
notice what falls out of the question when you do, and treat what fell out as
the thing you were really asking.
